## Content Pillars

Two pillars that are actually one topic: **how early life shapes your relationships.**

These aren't separate subjects mashed together — they're cause and effect. Childhood trauma directly wires how people behave in dating and relationships. The channel explores that connection from both ends.

### Pillar 1: Psychology of Trauma & Family Dysfunction
People search this privately, at midnight, trying to understand what happened to them. They're not asking friends. This is a deeply loyal, binge-watching audience.

Format: *"Here's what happened → here's what I didn't understand then → here's what I know now → here's what it might mean for you"*

Example titles:
- "Why children of chaotic homes can't relax when things are calm"
- "The specific way emotional abuse makes you distrust your own memory"
- "Growing up in chaos turned me into someone who can't stop scanning for problems"
- "Why your nervous system won't let you feel safe — even when you are"
- "The moment I realised my whole personality was a survival mechanism"

### Pillar 2: Dating & Relationships (Through a Psychological Lens)
Higher search volume, broader audience. Not everyone identifies as having trauma — but almost everyone has dating problems. This is the discovery engine that brings people in, then they find the trauma content and go "oh... that explains everything."

Example titles:
- "Why you keep choosing the wrong person — it's not bad luck"
- "The real reason you can't trust your partner (it started before you met them)"
- "Why you lose yourself in every relationship"
- "The anxious-avoidant trap: why it feels like love but isn't"
- "Why calm relationships feel boring to you"
- "You're not clingy — your nervous system is looking for safety"
- "Why you attract emotionally unavailable people (and how to stop)"

### How They Feed Each Other
The dating content brings in the broader audience (more searchable, less niche). The trauma content explains the *why* behind their patterns. Together they create a binge-watch loop:

- Someone finds: "Why you keep choosing the wrong person"
- They click next: "Growing up in chaos turned me into a scanner"
- Then: "Why your nervous system won't let you feel safe"
- Then they subscribe because nobody's ever connected these dots for them before

This is how you build watch time and subscriber loyalty — not from individual videos performing, but from people falling down the rabbit hole of your content.

## Video Format

**Length:** 7–10 minutes. Long enough to say something real, short enough to keep watch time percentage healthy.

**The only rule that matters:** Don't waste the first 30 seconds. YouTube measures hard whether people stay past 30 seconds. No "hey guys welcome to my channel." Start mid-thought:

> *"I spent 40 years not understanding why I couldn't relax when everything was fine. Turns out there's a specific reason for that..."*

Person scrolling stops. That's the goal.

## The Growth Reality — Hard Data (Don't Skip This)

### Realistic Timeline (Based on 2025-2026 Data)
- Average channel takes **22 months** to hit 1,000 subscribers without a strategy
- Channels that actively optimise titles/thumbnails/SEO cut that to **5-8 months**
- First 100 subscribers typically takes **8-12 weeks**
- **95% of YouTube videos** get fewer than 1,000 views
- **80% of channels** that start never reach monetisation — they quit before getting there
- 1,000 subscribers + 4,000 watch hours = monetisation threshold

### Money (When You Eventually Get There)
- Psychology/mental health CPM: **$5-15** per 1,000 ad impressions
- For comparison: finance is $15-30+, entertainment is $1-8
- A 10,000 subscriber channel in this niche might earn **$100-500/month**
- A 100,000 subscriber channel: **$1,000-5,000/month** (varies wildly)
- Long-form videos earn roughly **20x more** per view than Shorts
- Revenue is heavily skewed toward US/UK/AU viewers (your Aus audience is worth 3-5x more per view than viewers in developing countries)

### The Competition (Who You're Up Against)
- **Dr. Ramani** — narcissism/trauma, millions of subscribers
- **Patrick Teahan** — childhood trauma, hundreds of thousands of subscribers
- **Therapy in a Nutshell** — general psychology/mental health
- **Kati Morton** — licensed therapist, mental health
- **Dr. Tracey Marks** — psychiatrist, millions of subscribers
- **Psycho2Go** — animated psychology content

Most of these are credentialed therapists. Non-therapist lived-experience channels exist and grow, but slower because they don't have the "Dr." credibility shortcut. Your angle has to be clarity and rawness, not authority.

### What's Genuinely In Your Favour
- Trauma/psychology topics have **massive evergreen search volume** — people search "narcissistic parents," "childhood trauma signs," "hypervigilance" constantly
- These videos keep getting found **years after posting** (unlike trending/news content that dies in days)
- People search this stuff **privately** — they're not asking friends, they're typing it into YouTube at midnight
- The niche has proven, repeatable demand — it's not a gamble on whether people want this content

### What's Working Against You
- No credentials (not a therapist/psychologist)
- Zero existing audience
- Established creators have years of trust and content libraries built up
- You're starting from absolute zero algorithmic authority

### The 8-Views Reality
- Your first video got 8 views in 2 days on a zero-subscriber channel. That's completely normal. That's not failure.
- Deleting it was a mistake. Early videos aren't meant to perform — they're meant to exist and build signal over time.
- YouTube is a **library, not a feed**. Videos you post today get found in 6 months when your channel has authority.
- The first 20 videos are practice and data. Every one teaches you something.
- The creator you'll be at video 20 is unrecognisable compared to video 1.

**Don't check analytics on the first 20 videos.** Seriously. Checking views on a new channel is like weighing yourself after one day of eating well. Meaningless data that just demoralises you.

**Post frequency:** Once or twice a week. Consistency over months beats daily burnout over weeks.

### The Honest Bottom Line
The people who make it to video 50 are the ones who'd make videos even if nobody watched. Everyone else quits at video 8. The genuine interest in psychology is what gives you a chance — not some unique angle or clever strategy.

## What Doesn't Work (Lessons from StockIQ)

- AI-generated slop content — race to the bottom, platforms penalise it, audiences tune it out
- Multiple channels across different accounts — 3x the work, 3x slower growth on each, YouTube ToS risk
- Posting personal sadness without the "what I learned" frame — too raw for strangers who don't know you yet
- Random daily videos — chaos with a camera

**The vulnerability that works:** *"Here's what I went through + here's what I learned from it"*  
Not: *"Here's my pain"* — that distances.  
But: *"Here's what my pain taught me that you might recognise in yourself"* — that connects.
