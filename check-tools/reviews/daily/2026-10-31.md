## Faults (things that are wrong now)

### 1. The AI's daily calls on the sector funds are scored against the wrong S&P 500 price (sure)
`lambda-sync/stockiq-ai-trader/lambda_function.py`, `theme_read`, line 1658: the S&P 500 price stored with each call is looked for among
the 14 sector funds (`rows`), where SPY never is, so it always falls back to `th['spyNow']`. That is written only at the monthly change
(line 1709), last on 19 Oct. So every call made since 20 Oct is scored as "the fund's move from the day of the call" against "the S&P 500's
move from 19 Oct". Example: a call on 27 Oct scored on 3 Nov compares the fund over 27 Oct to 3 Nov with the S&P over 19 Oct to 3 Nov.
The S&P has moved about 2 to 3% in that time, which is as large as the moves being judged, so "right" and "wrong" are close to random.
**Why it matters for profit:** the notes say that at 30 scored calls a session decides whether the AI may lean the budget by its calls,
or stop being shown headlines. Today's "6 of 14 right" (Practice tab: "3 of 8" on 30 Oct) rests on this. Decided on these figures, it
would be a decision on noise.
**Fix:** in `run_themes`, quote SPY whenever the AI read is due (one more price request a day) and store that price with each call;
rescore the calls already made with SPY's daily close on each call's own day (the price function's `range=1mo`). Add a test in
`ai_trader_test.py` that a call made a week after the monthly change stores that day's S&P price. → Proposal on the Admin page.

### 2. The autopilot tab says "The AI model may sell early again" on Mon 2 Nov (sure)

(`AI_SELL = False`), so this is untrue; on Monday the check-in will also write "The AI model may sell early again: its pause is over."

working" and did not: teach it this phrase.

## The Reports tab

### 5. Reports are deleted after 3 days by default, but the 🎯 tracker measures 1 to 3 weeks after a report (sure)
- `dashboard.html` line 1167: the "Automatically delete analyses older than" menu starts on **3 days**; the nightly scheduler uses 3 days for
  anyone who never chose (`stockiq-auto-delete-scheduler/auto-delete-scheduler.py` line 30, `'3'`).
- The Top 10 tracker on the same tab shows "1 day later, 1 week later, 2 weeks later, 3 weeks later" (`TOP10_PERIODS`, line 3590). For
  anyone on the default, a report is gone before its "1 week later" column can fill. A trial visitor who comes back after the trial to
  see how their screener did finds nothing: the one feature that would show the screeners working over time is cut off by a default.
- There is no 30-day choice in the menu at all (1, 3, 7 days or never).
- The steering notes say reports are kept 30/90 days (S3's own rule); the table's rule is 3 days. The notes and the site disagree.

### 6. "Deleted" reports are never removed, so every daily cleanup and many dashboard loads read them again (fairly sure)
The cleanup only marks a report `deleted = True` (`stockiq-auto-delete-cleanup.py` line 106) and sets no expiry, while the dashboard's
own delete button sets both `deleted` and `ttl` (`stockiq-dashboard/lambda_function.py` line 649). So auto-deleted reports stay in the
table for good, and the cleanup's daily query reads all of them in full for every user, every day (a read of a report of 35 to 115 KB
costs 9 to 29 read units; asking for fewer fields does not lower that). This fits the table's 47,934 read units yesterday (the cost
check flags it). It grows by itself with every report ever run. The dashboard's history list (`get_user_history`) also pages through
deleted rows when a user has few live ones, which is slower. **Fix:** the cleanup sets the same `ttl` as the dashboard's delete (the
table removes the rows by itself, free), and queries only rows older than the cutoff (`timestamp < :cutoff` on the sort key). Whether TTL
is switched on for the table needs a look (`aws dynamodb describe-time-to-live`, free). Rows already marked deleted could be given a
`ttl` once: that removes users' data, so it needs the owner's yes. → Proposal on the Admin page, together with item 5.

Smaller, same tab: `loadAutoDeleteSetting()` is called twice on every dashboard load (lines 3416 and 3417), and with the dashboard's
own trial check and `auth.js` that is up to four calls to the trial function per load; one would do.
