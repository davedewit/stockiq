## Faults (first)

### 1. The "top 10 by trend" is often just the alphabet (sure)
- **What:** on the Russell 2000 page, **103 shares share the highest possible score, +2.7**, and the top 10 shown is the first 10
  of them by name: ABSI, ADPT, AGL, ALEC, AMLX, AMN, AMPL, ANF, AOUT, ATEX (every one starts with A). Seven of them carry "17 days
  in the top 10": true, but only because their names come early. On the S&P 500 page 11 shares tie at +2.7 and Valero (VLO) is
  left out of the top 10 for its name alone.
- **Why:** the trend score has a ceiling (2 + 1.5 + 0.5 + 0.5, times 0.6 = 2.7: `workers/stock_worker.py` lines 51-63), and
  in a year like this one many shares reach it. Then every ranking breaks the tie by name or list order:
  `update_screener_pages.py` line 390 sorts by score then symbol; the coordinator (`lambda_function.py` line 803) sorts by a score
  already rounded to one decimal, keeping list order; even the five-year study that chose the score (`scoring/wide_study.py`
  lines 85 and 93) kept tied shares in alphabetical order. The rounding makes it worse: AMPL (2.67) ranks above three shares at 2.70.
- **Why it matters for profit:** the autopilot buys from these ranks. On 20 Nov it bought MRNA ("rank 7, score +2.7") and PANW
  ("rank 9, score +2.7") from the S&P 500: their ranks were set by their names. The rank-pick trial (decided 6 Jan) compares the AI
  model's choice with "the top-ranked share", which among ties is the first name in the alphabet, so it is not testing what it says.
- **The change I would make:** replay a tie-break on the five-year prices first (`wide_study.py`): order shares with the same
  score by the uncapped rise over the year (then the uncapped distance above the 52-week low), against today's alphabet and a
  random order. If it holds in both halves, the worker sends the unrounded score and the tie-break figure, and the coordinator,
  the daily pages and the autopilot sort by them. Whatever the replay says, the daily pages should say plainly when more shares
  share the top score than the 10 shown ("103 shares share the highest score; these are 10 of them"). A scripted check
  (`site_audit.py`, say `top10-ties`) would catch this every day: a page whose 10th and 11th shares have the same score.
- Proposal on the Admin page (below).

### 2. Sign-ups scan the whole user table twice, on a table now limited to 2 read units (sure about the scans; likely cause of the throttling)
- **What:** the site check found **3 throttled requests yesterday on `stockiq-user-tracking`** (new problem). Since 20 Nov that
  table runs on 2 read and 2 write units of the free pool. Every registration runs `check_for_duplicate_trial`
  (`lambda-sync/stockiq-user-trial-manager/lambda_function.py` lines 443 and 453): **two full scans of the table** looking for the
  visitor's IP address. A scan reads every row, so one sign-up can use up the table's spare capacity and the page loads of other
  visitors that read the same table (the trial status, the usage count, the autopilot's plan check) are then throttled. The
  scans are also not paged: once the table is over 1 MB they only look at the first part of it.
- The check fails open (a failed check lets the registration through, line 598), so no sign-up is lost, but a visitor's usage
  chip can read "Status unavailable" and a free analysis can be counted wrongly (`daily-usage-tracker-anonymous.py` treats a failed
  read as 0).
- **The change:** replace the two scans with one query on the table's `ipAddress-index` (it already exists, with 1 unit), and
  give the functions that read this table the same patient retries the trader and portfolio got on 20 Nov (`AWS_MAX_ATTEMPTS`,
  `AWS_RETRY_MODE`). Costs nothing. The daily scans (the usage email, the user notification, the auto-delete scheduler) are once a
  day and can stay for now.
- Proposal on the Admin page (below).
