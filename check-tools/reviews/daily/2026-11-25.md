## Faults (first)

### 1. The home page's "Coming up" lists announcements that are already out (sure; found by asking "would this still be true a few hours later?")
- **Where:** `index.html` line 808, written by `update_screener_pages.py` lines 638-646. The writer takes the announcements "ahead" of
  *the moment it runs* (about 17:00-18:00 UTC) and the page's small script (line 809, and line 668 of the writer) only converts the
  times to the visitor's zone: it never looks at whether a time has gone by.
- **Evidence now:** the page says "Coming up: Wed 25 Nov, 00:30 UTC Australia: inflation (month) expected 0.4% · last time 0.8%",
  and the same for inflation (year) and Trimmed Mean CPI. At 07:00 UTC those are 6.5 hours old. A visitor in Sydney or Tokyo, who
  reads the page in the morning that follows each run, always sees the Australian and Asian announcements of that morning as "coming
  up", with a forecast and no result. The Mac is often off for days (the notes say so), and then the whole list can be days old and
  still headed "Coming up". The 13:30 UTC US items (Core PCE, GDP) are right until they happen, then wrong in the same way.
- **Why it matters:** this is the line on the home page that is meant to show the site is alive today. A past event under "Coming
  up" tells a returning visitor the opposite, and "expected 0.4%" for something already published is a figure that is no longer news.
- **The change:** in the page's own small script, compare each `<time datetime>` with the visitor's clock: hide the ones more than
  an hour past, or move them under a muted "Just out" with "expected ... · last time ..." kept; if none are left, show the writer's
  "Out this week" wording. No new request, no cost, no page weight. A scripted check: the script run against a stubbed clock in
  `site_audit.py`'s `time-claims` (the audit itself runs straight after the update, when everything is still ahead, so it cannot
  see this by looking at the live page: it needs the clock stubbed). Proposal on the Admin page.

### 2. The daily usage email scans the whole user table twice, at the hour the table throttles, and reads only its first 1 MB (likely cause of the throttling; sure about the code)
- **Evidence:** the site check again says "table stockiq-user-tracking: 3 throttled requests yesterday" (24 Nov; also 23 Nov), after
  the units were raised on 22 Nov. It is the same count every day. The watch list put the throttles at "about 17:00 UTC on 20 and 21
  Nov". `stockiq-usage-report-emailed` (`daily_anonymous_analysis_report.py` lines 22 and 31) runs at 03:00 Sydney time (notes:
  `lambda-reference.md`), which is 16:00 to 17:00 UTC, and makes **two full `scan()` calls** on that table, one for anonymous
  visitors and one for logged-in ones. A scan reads every row whatever its filter, in one burst.
- **A second fault in the same code (sure about the code, not about the table's size):** neither scan follows `LastEvaluatedKey`, so
  each reads at most the first 1 MB page of the table. If the table is bigger than 1 MB the daily email's counts of anonymous and
  logged-in analyses are too low and nobody would see it. One free command (`aws dynamodb describe-table`, TableSizeBytes) says
  which; I cannot run it here.
- **Yesterday's report said the cause is #34 (the sign-up scans).** #34 may add to it, but sign-ups are a few a day and would not
  give exactly three every day at the same hour. I think the daily email is the cause; CloudWatch's `ReadThrottleEvents` per minute
  for 23 Nov (free) would show whether the throttles sit at the report's minute.
- **The change:** one scan with both filters and a loop on `LastEvaluatedKey`, and the patient-retry settings the autopilot and
  portfolio functions got on 20 Nov (`AWS_MAX_ATTEMPTS=20`, adaptive). That also clears the site check's one standing problem, which is
  what keeps the Admin page at "When you can" every day. Proposal on the Admin page; #34 stays as it is.
