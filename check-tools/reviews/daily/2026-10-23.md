## Faults (first)

### 1. The unsubscribe page only pretends to unsubscribe, and sends the address to Google Analytics (sure)
- `website/unsubscribe.html` lines 141-160: "Simulate unsubscribe (replace with actual API call later)". It waits a second and
  shows "Successfully Unsubscribed". Nothing is sent to the list. The function behind the list
  (`lambda-sync/stockiq-email-capture/email-capture-with-count.py`) has only `subscribe` and `get_count`: there is no way to
  unsubscribe at all.
- The same code sends the visitor's email address to Google Analytics as part of an event (`gtag('event', 'unsubscribe',
  {'email': email})`, line 155). Google's terms forbid sending personal details such as email addresses to Analytics.
- The home page box promises "Unsubscribe anytime" (`index.html` line 976). Today no email is sent, so nobody is harmed yet.
  But the traffic plan's step 4 is a weekly email to this list, and an email with an unsubscribe that does not work breaks
  Australia's Spam Act. This has to be right before the first email goes out.
- **Fix:** an `unsubscribe` action in the function (sets `status` to `unsubscribed`; the subscribe code already handles a
  returning address), the page calling it for real and showing what it answers, the email address taken out of the
  Analytics event. Put on the Admin page as a proposal.

### 2. The home page asks a third-party site for the visitor's address on every signup (sure)
- `index.html` line 2092: before saving a signup the page asks `api.ipify.org` for the visitor's IP address. The function does
  not need it: it uses the real address from the request first (`source_ip`, line 41 and 62 of the function). So it is one
  outside service told about every visitor who signs up, a short delay, and nothing gained. The privacy policy does not
  mention it.
- **Fix:** take the ipify request out. Also the older `captureEmail` at `index.html` line 1452 is dead code (the second one at
  line 2067 replaces it) and writes the email address into the browser console: remove it. Both go with fault 1.

### 3. Shares the trial bought by rank are recorded as "chosen by the AI model" (sure; small)
- Since 23 Oct, every second share the model picks is swapped for the top-ranked share (`apply_rank_pick`, the `rank-pick`
  trial). The buy is still remembered with `chosen: 'ai'` (`remember_buy`, line 1689; the swap only adds `rp: 1`, line 2668).
- So "By who chose" in the breakdown, and the line the AI model itself is shown ("chosen by the AI model: N trades …",
  `memory_text`), will count shares it did not choose. The trial's own comparison (by `rp`) is right.
- **Fix:** record `chosen: 'rank'` when `rp` is 1. One line. Part of proposal 2.

### 4. The review tool calls every automatic budget change "a change of the settings" (sure; small)
- `review-2026-10-23.txt` part 1 lists 33 changes in 4 days, almost all of them the budget following results (`follow_results`
  changes `budgetUsd` at most check-ins), and then warns "the settings changed several times; trades made under different
  settings are not one experiment". That warning is wrong here: the budget following results is one setting, not a new
  experiment each time.
- **Fix:** in `check-tools/autopilot_review.py`, leave out changes of `budgetUsd` that match a "The budget is now …" line of the
  same minute (or count them in one line). Report-tool only.

## What would bring visitors, sign-ups or profit

### 5. The learning loop learns mostly from a score that is no longer used, and from groups too small to read (fairly sure)
- The screeners' score was replaced on 17 Oct (the trend score). The notes say the trades before and after are different
  experiments and must never be pooled; about 15 of the 98 finished trades were bought under the new score. But the shared
  record pools them all: the scorecard the model is shown, the notes it writes (`write_lessons`), the rule proposals
  (`propose`) and the screener resting (`review_resting`) all read every trade together.
- Today's note (11:10 UTC), which the model now reads at every choice, rests on that mix and on groups below 30 trades, and
  contradicts itself: "Trades ranked 4 to 10 (24 trades) performed best … high RSI and rank led to better outcomes, but many
  trades with low ranks performed poorly." The scorecard lines it is shown start at 8 trades a group (`MIN_SAMPLE`).
- **The resting rule needs a replay before the AI/Manual screener mode builds on it.** `review_resting` rests a screener for
  14 days when its last 8 to 12 trades average 1.5% behind the S&P 500 fund. The study of 23 Oct
  (`scoring/market_trend_study.py`) found that leaving a market because its picks lagged the S&P for 12 weeks did not pass
  (1 of 9 lists, then 7 of 9). The resting rule is the same idea on even fewer trades, and one bad night (six Asian shares
  through their loss limits on 22-23 Oct) could rest a screener on one correlated event. The next session's first job (AI or
  Manual for the screener ticks) is to rely on exactly this rule.
- **Change:** tag every remembered buy with the score version; let the scorecard, the notes, `propose` and the resting rule
  read only trades of the current score (or show the two apart); the notes and the model's scorecard lines from 30 trades a
  group; count trades closed in the same session in one market as one when resting; replay `review_resting` on the wide price
  set (both halves) before the AI mode leans on it. Proposal 2.
